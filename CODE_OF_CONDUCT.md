# Code of Conduct

KyrgyzSTEM is built for high-school students, many of them under 18, by volunteers,
teachers and developers. This code applies to everyone who takes part in the project:
in issues, pull requests, reviews, discussions, chats, events, and on the platform
itself. It is written for this project, inspired by the
[Contributor Covenant](https://www.contributor-covenant.org/).

## Our standards

We expect everyone to:

* be respectful and patient, in any of the project's languages (Kyrgyz, Russian,
  English) and at any level of experience;
* give and accept constructive criticism of work, not of people;
* assume good intent, and ask before assuming;
* credit others' work, and respect licenses and authorship;
* make space for beginners: every olympiad champion started with their first problem.

Not acceptable:

* harassment, insults, threats, or discriminatory jokes or language (including about
  ethnicity, nationality, language, religion, gender, sexual orientation, disability,
  age, appearance or economic background);
* sexual language or imagery of any kind;
* publishing anyone's private information (addresses, phone numbers, school, photos)
  without explicit permission;
* plagiarism: submitting someone else's problems, solutions or code as your own;
* sustained disruption of discussions, or deliberately misleading others.

## Protecting students

Because students are minors, these rules are stricter than in most open-source projects
and apply without exception:

* **No private contact with students** through or because of the project. Adults do not
  ask students for private chats, phone numbers, social-media accounts or meetings.
  Mentoring happens in public project spaces or through the student's school.
* **Do not ask for or share students' personal data** (full names, schools, photos,
  results), in issues, PRs, screenshots, test data or anywhere else. Use synthetic data.
* **Do not access student data** beyond what your role in an organization requires, and
  never copy it out of the platform.
* If you see something that suggests a student is at risk, report it (below) and, where
  appropriate, to the student's school or local authorities.

## Enforcement

Maintainers may edit or remove comments, commits, issues and other contributions that
break this code, and may warn, temporarily ban or permanently ban anyone from the
project spaces, depending on severity. Breaking the student-protection rules leads to an
immediate ban and, where the law requires, a report to the authorities.

## Reporting

Report problems to the maintainers through the repository's private reporting form
(Security tab, "Report a vulnerability"). Reports are handled confidentially; we will tell you what we did, and we
will not reveal who reported. If the report concerns a maintainer, that person takes no
part in handling it.

For security vulnerabilities, follow [docs/SECURITY.md](docs/SECURITY.md) instead.
